# World brief: Corvane Group

Corvane Group is fictional. It exists to give Omni a realistic, messy,
twenty-year organisation to ingest. No real company, person or site is
depicted. Country and city names are real places used only as locations.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Business | Integrated resources, energy and logistics |
| People | About 18,500 employees plus contractors |
| Footprint | About 140 sites in 14 countries |
| Global head office | Perth, Australia |
| Regional headquarters | Singapore (Asia), Houston (Americas), Rotterdam (Europe and Africa) |
| Founded | 2006, so the archive runs 2006 to 2026 |

## Structure

Four operating divisions and one corporate division. Each division has
regional business units; each business unit has sites.

| Division | What it does | Typical sites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resources | Mining and mineral processing | Mines, processing plants, camps |
| Energy | Gas and liquids pipelines, storage, terminals | Pipeline segments, compressor stations, terminals |
| Logistics | Road, rail and sea freight; warehousing | Depots, rail yards, ports, warehouses |
| Infrastructure Services | Maintenance, construction, shutdowns | Workshops, project offices |
| Corporate | Finance, People, Legal, Risk and Compliance, Technology, Procurement | Head office and regional headquarters |

## Why it resembles a defence force

- A head office, regional commands, and hundreds of dispersed units.
- A workforce whose right to work depends on current safety certifications,
  medicals and inductions, which expire.
- Large fleets (haul trucks, locomotives, vessels, light vehicles) with
  maintenance schedules and spare-parts chains.
- Long supply lines to remote sites, with fuel, parts and consumables.
- Long-lived infrastructure with inspection regimes.
- Operations planned as projects and campaigns with dependencies.
- Heavy regulation, incident reporting and audits.

## Twenty-year timeline

| Year | Event | Effect on the data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2006 | Founded as a regional freight company | One small finance system, spreadsheets |
| 2009 | Enters mining services | First maintenance system |
| 2011 | Acquires a pipeline operator | A second, incompatible asset register |
| 2013 | First overseas region (Asia) | Multi-currency ledgers begin |
| 2015 | Group-wide HR system replaces three local ones | People schema changes; duplicates appear |
| 2016 | Commodity downturn, restructure | Business units renamed and merged |
| 2018 | Acquires a European logistics firm | Third set of site and fleet codes |
| 2020 | Pandemic disruption | Gaps, late reports, emergency procurement |
| 2021 | Single ERP rollout begins | Old and new systems overlap for three years |
| 2023 | Major pipeline incident and inquiry | Surge of legal, risk and audit documents |
| 2025 | Northern pipeline expansion approved | The live project used in the demo |

## Source systems the archive imitates

| Source | Covers | Form | Mess to include |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Finance ledgers (two generations) | Budgets, spend, invoices | Database exports | Chart of accounts changed in 2021 |
| HR system (old and new) | People, roles, certifications, training | Database exports | Duplicate people after the 2015 merge |
| Maintenance systems (three) | Assets, work orders, faults, parts used | Database exports | Three coding schemes for the same asset types |
| Inventory and procurement | Stock, orders, suppliers, contracts | Tables and emailed spreadsheets | Conflicting stock counts |
| Operations | Shift reports, project plans, movement logs | Free text and spreadsheets | Late and missing entries |
| Legal and risk | Contracts, incident reports, audit findings | Scanned PDFs and documents | Poor scans, inconsistent naming |
| Site register | Sites, routes, pipeline segments | Geospatial files | Renamed and closed sites |

## Problems to plant (each with an answer key)

- **Supply and Logistics:** a critical spare part whose consumption will halt a
  haul fleet within weeks; a supplier whose lead times have been quietly
  lengthening for a year.
- **People:** a wave of safety certifications expiring at one site shortly
  before a planned shutdown; rising turnover in one trade at remote sites.
- **Operations:** the northern pipeline expansion depends on a crew and a
  crane that two other projects have also booked.
- **Finance:** a maintenance contract trending well over budget across
  three cost centres that nobody views together.
- **Assets:** a pump model failing at twice the rate at coastal sites.
- **Risk and Compliance:** three incident reports from different sites
  that describe the same underlying fault.
